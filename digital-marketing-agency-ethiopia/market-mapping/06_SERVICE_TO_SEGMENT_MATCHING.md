# Service-to-Segment Matching

## Purpose

[01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md](01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md) identifies who could buy; [02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md](02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md) ranks who to approach first. This file answers a different question: **once you're in front of a given business type, which services from [../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md](../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md) should you actually recommend?**

The core discipline here is the same one stated in [../01_AGENCY_STRATEGY_AND_POSITIONING.md](../01_AGENCY_STRATEGY_AND_POSITIONING.md) §10: recommend the smallest package that produces a measurable outcome for that specific business type, not the largest package the agency is capable of selling. A "Very High" rating below means "this service is usually central to solving this segment's core problem," not "always sell the most expensive version of it."

Ratings are qualitative judgment calls (Low / Medium / High / Very High), the same way as the illustrative scores in the prioritization file — recalibrate per real client based on their specific situation during [operations/04_CLIENT_DISCOVERY.md](../operations/04_CLIENT_DISCOVERY.md), not applied mechanically.

## Why Service Priority Differs by Industry

The matrix isn't arbitrary — four underlying dynamics explain most of the variation:

### 1. How the customer discovers the business

- **Proximity-driven discovery** (restaurants, salons, laundromats, home services, auto repair): the customer is nearby and choosing among visible local options right now. Google Business Profile and Maps visibility dominate; a large website adds little at this stage.
- **Research-driven discovery** (hotels, law firms, real estate, clinics, schools): the customer compares multiple options over days or weeks before deciding. A credible website and consistent content matter more because the customer is actively evaluating, not just picking the nearest option.
- **Referral/trust-network discovery** (construction contractors, accountants, B2B manufacturers): the customer relies on word-of-mouth or professional networks more than open search. Website and LinkedIn presence matter for validating a referral, not generating one from cold search.
- **Platform/marketplace discovery** (e-commerce and social-commerce sellers, some retail): the customer discovers and buys inside a social or marketplace platform rather than via general search. Social media and paid ads dominate; a standalone website is secondary or purely supportive.

### 2. What builds trust before purchase

- **Peer reviews** are the primary trust signal for experience-based or health/safety-relevant services (restaurants, clinics, salons, hotels, auto repair) — hence "Very High" review importance across most consumer-facing segments.
- **Portfolio and credentials** matter more than review volume for professional and creative services (law, architecture, construction, IT) — website case studies and LinkedIn presence carry more weight than star ratings.
- **Regulatory/brand trust** dominates for financial services and healthcare, where testimonial and rating-based marketing is restricted or less persuasive than credentials, licensing, and compliance signals.

### 3. Value and frequency of a single transaction

- **High-frequency, low-value** purchases (cafés, bakeries, laundromats) justify lower-cost, always-on local visibility work rather than expensive paid-ad spend per customer.
- **Low-frequency, high-value** purchases (real estate, cars, weddings, B2B contracts) justify heavier investment in lead-quality tools (CRM, tracked forms, a stronger website) because losing one lead has a real cost.
- **Subscription/repeat-relationship** businesses (gyms, schools, clinics with ongoing care) benefit disproportionately from CRM and retention-focused messaging (email/SMS/WhatsApp) because the value is in the ongoing relationship, not the first visit.

### 4. Regulatory and structural constraints

- Healthcare, financial services, and government segments carry compliance overhead (see the ⚠️ flags throughout `market-mapping/` and `operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md`) that limits how aggressively paid advertising, testimonials, and outcome claims can be used, independent of what would otherwise be effective.
- B2B and export-oriented segments (manufacturers, exporters) sell to other businesses or international buyers, not local consumers — so Google Business Profile and reviews matter far less than a credible website, LinkedIn presence, and direct outreach/CRM discipline.

## How to Use This File

1. During [operations/04_CLIENT_DISCOVERY.md](../operations/04_CLIENT_DISCOVERY.md), identify the prospect's segment and pull its row from the matrix as a starting hypothesis, not a final answer.
2. Confirm or adjust the hypothesis against the specific business's actual situation (existing assets, budget, target customer) before proposing a package from [../commercial/01_SERVICE_PACKAGES.md](../commercial/01_SERVICE_PACKAGES.md).
3. Use the "Primary Objective" column to keep the proposal and eventual reporting anchored to the outcome that actually matters for that business, per [operations/11_REPORTING_AND_BUSINESS_REVIEWS.md](../operations/11_REPORTING_AND_BUSINESS_REVIEWS.md).

## Standard Operating Procedure — Maintaining This Matrix

- [ ] When a new segment is added to `01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md`, add a corresponding row here before it is actively sold to.
- [ ] If real client outcomes repeatedly contradict a row's ratings (e.g. paid ads consistently underperform for a segment rated "High"), update the row and note why.
- [ ] Keep ratings qualitative (Low/Medium/High/Very High) — do not attach invented numeric benchmarks to this file.
- [ ] Re-check ⚠️-flagged rows whenever compliance guidance in `operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md` changes.
