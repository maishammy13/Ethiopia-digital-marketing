# Ethiopia Customer Segment Map (Level 1: Customer Types)

## Purpose and scope

This file maps the types of Ethiopian organizations that could plausibly buy one or more services from [../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md](../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md). It is deliberately broad: the goal is to see the whole addressable market before narrowing it, not to jump straight to a shortlist.

This is **Level 1 mapping only** — sectors, sub-sectors, and generic business types. It does not name real companies. For how to turn this into a list of actual, named Ethiopian businesses to contact, see [07_PROSPECTING_DATABASE_STRUCTURE.md](07_PROSPECTING_DATABASE_STRUCTURE.md) (Level 2).

For which of these segments to pursue first, see [02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md](02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md). For which services fit which segment, see [06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md](06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md).

**No market-size, revenue, or population statistics are stated in this file.** Category notes describe general, qualitative characteristics only. Any quantitative claim about a sector's size or growth in Ethiopia must be researched and sourced separately before use in strategy or sales materials.

**Regulated or sensitive sectors are flagged inline with ⚠️.** These require compliance review under [operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md) before marketing services are sold or delivered, particularly around advertising claims, testimonials, and data handling.

## S. Religious and Community-Focused Businesses and Organizations

This category includes service providers and organizations connected to religious and community life (event and venue services, printing and media for religious organizations, community-focused retailers, etc.). Handle with particular care:

- Marketing must be culturally and religiously sensitive — avoid generic "hard-sell" advertising language and imagery that would read as inappropriate in this context.
- Trust-based, relationship-driven communication (referrals, community leader endorsement, in-person relationship building) is typically more effective than cold outreach or paid advertising for this category.
- Confirm decision-making structure early — purchasing decisions are frequently made by a committee or leadership body rather than one individual, which extends the sales cycle.
- Any advertising involving religious institutions directly (as opposed to businesses serving that community) should be treated with extra scrutiny and is generally lower priority for a new agency's core packages.

## T. Additional Segments Worth Tracking

Segments not cleanly captured above that are worth watching as the agency matures, added here rather than invented into false precision:

- **Diaspora-facing businesses** — Ethiopian businesses whose customer base includes the diaspora (import/export, remittance-adjacent services, cultural goods, travel services) — these often need English-language content and international payment/logistics messaging alongside domestic marketing.
- **Co-working and shared office spaces** — an emerging urban commercial category with both B2B (tenant acquisition) and event-hosting marketing needs.
- **Vocational trade cooperatives and artisan collectives** (weavers, potters, leather artisans) — often underserved digitally, high visual-content potential, may need group/cooperative-level rather than single-business marketing.
- **Franchises and multi-location local chains** — a distinct operational category (see multi-location handling in [market-mapping/03_CITY_AND_GEOGRAPHIC_PRIORITIZATION.md](03_CITY_AND_GEOGRAPHIC_PRIORITIZATION.md)) requiring consistent branding across locations with local-level Google Business Profile and review management per site.
- **B2B software/service resellers and system integrators** — distinct from Section R's product companies; sell implementation and support services rather than software itself.

Revisit this section periodically — if a segment here proves to have real, repeatable demand, promote it into its own lettered section with the same level of business-type detail as A–S.

## How to Use This Map

1. Do not attempt to sell to all 20 sectors at once. Use [02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md](02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md) to score and rank segments.
2. For a chosen segment, use [06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md](06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md) to determine which services in [../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md](../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md) are actually appropriate.
3. To find real, named businesses within a prioritized segment, follow the research procedure in [07_PROSPECTING_DATABASE_STRUCTURE.md](07_PROSPECTING_DATABASE_STRUCTURE.md).
4. Before selling into any ⚠️-flagged sector, complete the relevant compliance check in [operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md).
