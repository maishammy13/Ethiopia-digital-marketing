# Offer Tiers

## Purpose

This is the actual "menu" the agency shows a client — three tiers, simple enough to choose from in one conversation, backed by the full 26-service catalogue without ever needing to expose that full complexity to someone who just wants to know what they get and what it costs. No client should ever be handed the raw [../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md](../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md) and asked to assemble their own package — that is the agency's job, done once here so every salesperson presents the same three, well-designed options.

**The tiers differ in responsibility, speed, personalization, certainty, service depth, support, and reporting — never in random feature additions.** Every tier is built directly from the Time Delay and Customer Effort levers in [01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md](01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md) Sections 4–5: each step up removes more of the client's time, effort, coordination burden, and uncertainty. That is the entire logic of the ladder — not "more stuff," but "less required of you, more certainty, faster."

**Every tier must be genuinely, independently valuable.** A lower tier is simpler and requires more from the client — it must never be deliberately crippled or made unusable just to force an upgrade. See the ethical requirements in [01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md](01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md) Section 16.

Exactly which services from the catalogue populate each tier, per individual service category (website, GBP, social, lead generation, etc.), is worked out in [04_SERVICE_PACKAGE_MATRIX.md](04_SERVICE_PACKAGE_MATRIX.md). This file defines the three tiers themselves — their principle, their characteristics, and the relative pricing logic between them.

## Tier 1: Foundation ("Do It Yourself")

**What it is.** The agency provides knowledge, templates, checklists, and a limited amount of hands-on setup. The client performs most of the actual implementation and ongoing work.

**Possible components:**
- A digital-presence audit identifying the client's specific gaps
- Website or social-media templates suited to their segment
- A Google Business Profile setup checklist
- A content-calendar template
- Review-request templates and scripts
- Recorded or written how-to guidance
- One strategy consultation
- Limited technical support (e.g. a defined number of support requests, not open-ended)

**Characteristics:**
- Lowest price
- Highest client effort
- Lowest agency labor per client
- Limited customization — templates and frameworks, not bespoke work
- Limited, defined support (not continuous availability)
- Best suited to highly price-sensitive clients
- Best suited to businesses with internal staff who have the time and some capability to implement

**What the client must do:** implement the guidance themselves — populate the website template, follow the GBP checklist personally, schedule and post their own content, request their own reviews, and reach out to the agency only within the defined support allowance. The agency's role here is expert direction, not execution.

**Who this is genuinely right for:** an owner-operator with time but limited budget, or a business with an in-house person (even part-time) capable of following clear instructions. It is not the right fit for a business with no one available to actually do the implementation work — steering that prospect toward Foundation anyway, knowing they won't execute, would produce a client who fails and blames the agency. Recommend the tier that matches their real capacity, per the segment-fit discipline in [../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md](../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md).

## Tier 2: Guided Growth ("Done With You")

**What it is.** The agency and client share implementation responsibility. The agency does the strategic and higher-skill work; the client handles defined, bounded pieces of ongoing execution with agency guidance and partial support.

**Possible components:**
- Full strategy development (not a template — built for this specific business)
- Account setup (GBP, social, tracking) done by the agency
- Website structure and design guidance, built by the agency
- A content plan and calendar, built by the agency
- Ready-to-use templates for the pieces the client will execute themselves
- A monthly consultation/check-in
- A monthly performance review
- Staff training on the specific tasks the client will own (see [../services/26_ADDITIONAL_SERVICES.md](../services/26_ADDITIONAL_SERVICES.md) Item 9)
- Partial implementation — the agency executes the harder or higher-stakes pieces, the client executes the routine, ongoing pieces
- A defined, limited number of revisions per deliverable

**Characteristics:**
- Medium-to-high price
- Moderate client effort
- Shared responsibility, clearly divided (see the responsibility table below)
- More guidance, more customization than Foundation
- Best suited to businesses with some internal marketing or administrative capacity, but not enough to do everything, or not wanting to do everything

**This tier must be commercially valid and genuinely useful on its own — not a deliberately weak middle option that exists only to make Managed Growth look better by comparison.** A client who selects Guided Growth and never upgrades should still get real, working results. What must be shown clearly, and honestly, is exactly which responsibilities remain with the client (see the responsibility table below) — that is the legitimate differentiator from Managed Growth, not withheld quality.

## Tier 3: Managed Growth ("Done For You")

**What it is.** The premium tier. The agency performs nearly all execution across the services in scope. This is the tier built most directly around the Customer Effort and Sacrifice logic in [01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md](01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md) Section 5 — it exists specifically to give the client their time and attention back.

**Possible components:**
- Complete, bespoke strategy
- Website development, design, and copywriting
- Google Business Profile management, full-service
- SEO (technical, on-page, local, and off-page as relevant)
- Photography and video production
- Social-media publishing and community management
- Review management (generation, monitoring, response)
- Advertising management across relevant platforms
- Lead tracking and CRM integration
- Analytics and honest, regular reporting
- Priority support and response times
- Regular (e.g. monthly or quarterly) strategy meetings

**Characteristics:**
- Highest price
- Lowest client effort
- Greatest agency responsibility
- Fastest implementation (see the Time Delay levers in [01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md](01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md) Section 4)
- Highest customization
- Most direct, most responsive support
- Strongest internal coordination (a single point of contact managing everything behind the scenes — see [../operations/08_PROJECT_MANAGEMENT.md](../operations/08_PROJECT_MANAGEMENT.md))
- Most complete measurement and reporting (built on [../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md](../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md))
- Best suited to businesses that value time, convenience, certainty, and execution over doing it themselves — regardless of whether that's because of capacity, skill, or simple preference

**What the client primarily needs to do, even at this tier:**
- Provide access (accounts, credentials, physical locations for photography — see [../operations/07_ACCESS_AND_ASSET_COLLECTION.md](../operations/07_ACCESS_AND_ASSET_COLLECTION.md))
- Provide accurate business information
- Approve major decisions (brand direction, budget changes, significant strategy shifts)
- Handle incoming customers or leads once they arrive (the agency delivers the inquiry; closing the sale in person or over the phone remains the client's business)
- Deliver the actual product or service to their own customers

**What this tier deliberately does not promise:** it does not promise outcomes outside the agency's control (see [01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md](01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md) Section 3) — even the most complete, most expensive tier guarantees deliverables and effort, never a guaranteed ranking, revenue figure, or follower count.

## Responsibility Comparison Across Tiers

This table is the clearest, most honest way to show what actually differs between tiers — not a feature list, but who does the work.

| Task | Foundation | Guided Growth | Managed Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Agency-provided template/framework | Jointly developed with the agency | Fully customized, developed by the agency |
| Website/account setup | Client, using agency guidance | Agency sets up; client populates some content | Fully built and populated by the agency |
| Photography | Client | Shared — agency for key assets, client for routine ones | Agency |
| Copywriting | Client, using templates | Joint — agency drafts, client provides input | Agency |
| Publishing/posting | Client | Shared — client posts routine content, agency handles key content | Agency |
| Review management | Client, using templates and scripts | Client requests reviews; agency advises on response | Agency (generation process + response) |
| Advertising | Not included, or client self-manages with guidance | Agency sets up; client can manage day-to-day with support | Agency, fully managed |
| Monitoring | Client, limited agency check-ins | Monthly agency review | Continuous agency monitoring |
| Reporting | Basic — a template the client fills in themselves, or none | Standard monthly report | Detailed, honest monthly report plus quarterly strategy review |
| Support | Limited, defined allowance | Scheduled monthly consultation | Priority, ongoing support |
| Client effort overall | High | Medium | Low |
| Agency responsibility overall | Limited | Shared | Extensive |

A fuller, client-facing version of this table — styled for presentation, not internal planning — is built in [08_PRICING_PRESENTATION.md](08_PRICING_PRESENTATION.md).

## Relative Price Architecture

The gap between tiers is not an arbitrary percentage chosen to make the top tier look attractive — it should reflect the real difference in agency labor, client effort removed, service depth, and value created, calculated using the pricing formula in [02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md](02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md).

**Illustrative reference only** — not a fixed rule:

| Tier | Relative price | Client effort |
|---|---:|---:|
| Foundation | 10–25% of the Managed Growth price | High |
| Guided Growth | 55–80% of the Managed Growth price | Medium |
| Managed Growth | 100% (reference point) | Low |

**Why Foundation is a small fraction of the top tier:** it contains comparatively little agency labor — templates, a checklist, and one consultation, reused with minor adaptation across many clients. Its cost floor (per [02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md](02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md) Perspective A) is genuinely low.

**Why Guided Growth sits materially closer to Managed Growth than to Foundation:** it includes real strategy work, real account setup, and partial execution — the harder, more skilled portion of the work is often already agency-delivered at this tier, even though ongoing routine execution is shared. The jump from Foundation to Guided Growth is where most of the agency's real labor enters; the jump from Guided Growth to Managed Growth is comparatively about *taking over the remaining client-held tasks* and adding speed, certainty, and support.

**Why Managed Growth can justify a substantial premium over Guided Growth:** it removes essentially all remaining client effort, adds the fastest implementation, the most support, and the most complete measurement — the full Time-Delay and Customer-Effort value described in [01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md](01_VALUE_ARCHITECTURE.md).

**Rule:** these percentages are a design reference for sanity-checking a proposed price structure, not a formula to apply mechanically. The final price for each tier must be derived from the actual scope, delivery cost, agency capacity, risk, and customer value calculated per [02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md](02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md) and [06_PRICING_CALCULATOR.md](06_PRICING_CALCULATOR.md) — never inflate Guided Growth's price artificially just to make Managed Growth look like the obvious choice by comparison. The price difference between any two tiers must be commercially defensible on its own terms: if challenged to justify it line by line, the agency should be able to.
