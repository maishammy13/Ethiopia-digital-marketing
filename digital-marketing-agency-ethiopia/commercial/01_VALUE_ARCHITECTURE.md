# Value Architecture

## Purpose

Before packaging or pricing anything, the agency needs a precise, shared understanding of *why* a customer would pay at all. This file defines that logic. Every package in [03_OFFER_TIERS.md](03_OFFER_TIERS.md) and every price in [02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md](02_PRICING_ARCHITECTURE.md) traces back to the value equation defined here.

**Ground rule for this entire file and the rest of `commercial/`:** no invented revenue figures, no fabricated Ethiopian market statistics, and no A/B testing or pricing-experiment content — this file constructs the value architecture, it does not test it. Where a number is needed and no reliable figure exists, use a qualitative Low/Medium/High/Very High rating with stated reasoning, the same discipline used in [../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md](../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md).

## 1. The Value Equation

```
Value = (Desired Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Success) ÷ (Time Delay × Customer Effort and Sacrifice)
```

This is the core lens for every commercial decision the agency makes. Increasing the numerator (a bigger, more certain outcome) or decreasing the denominator (faster, easier) both increase the value a customer perceives — and both are levers the agency actually controls, unlike the customer's underlying willingness to pay.

Each component is expanded below in practical digital-marketing terms, then applied to pricing logic throughout the rest of `commercial/`.

## 2. Desired Outcome

### Customers don't buy deliverables — they buy outcomes

A customer does not fundamentally want a website, an Instagram calendar, a set of SEO fixes, a Google Business Profile, an ad campaign, a video, or a report. These are **delivery mechanisms** — the agency's means of production, not the reason the customer pays.

What a customer actually wants is closer to:

- More telephone calls
- More WhatsApp inquiries
- More appointments or bookings
- More store visits
- More restaurant customers
- More qualified leads
- More sales
- Greater trust with prospective customers
- Better online visibility relative to competitors
- More positive reviews
- Stronger brand recognition
- Lower dependence on referrals or intermediaries (e.g. booking platforms, delivery apps) that take a cut
- More predictable, repeatable customer acquisition instead of feast-or-famine
- More direct bookings instead of paying commission to a third-party platform
- More repeat customers, not just first-time ones

**Do not present agency activities as though they are the final value**, in a sales conversation, a proposal, or a report. "We posted 12 times this month" is an activity. "You received 34 more WhatsApp inquiries than last month" is an outcome. The difference is the entire basis of what a customer is willing to pay for.

### The five-level value chain

For every service, trace the full chain from what the agency actually does to what it's ultimately worth to the business:

| Level | Definition | Example (Google Business Profile) | Example (Website) | Example (Social Media Management) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Activity | The task the agency performs | Optimize a Google Business Profile | Build and launch a website | Publish and manage social content |
| Deliverable | The tangible thing produced | A completed, optimized business listing | A live, working website | Published posts, managed community |
| Immediate output | The direct, measurable platform-level result | More search and Maps visibility, more profile views | More sessions, more organic traffic | More reach, more engagement |
| Customer outcome | What actually changes for the business | More calls, direction requests, and walk-in visits | More qualified inquiries and bookings | More inbound messages that convert to sales |
| Economic value | The financial/strategic result | More paying customers and revenue; reduced dependence on paid ads for visibility | More revenue at a lower cost per acquisition than paid channels alone | More revenue and stronger brand equity that compounds over time |

This five-level structure extends the four-level Activity → Output → Outcome → Financial-impact table already used in every `services/` file's Section B (see, for example, [../services/10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md](../services/10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md) Section B) by splitting "Output" into the platform-level immediate output and the real customer-level outcome. Use this five-level version specifically in sales conversations and value-focused proposal language — the four-level version in each service file remains the internal reference for KPI design.

**Rule for every proposal, pitch, or report:** lead with customer outcome and economic value, not activity or deliverable. A client should never have to translate "we optimized your categories and posted weekly" into "so what does that get me?" — the agency does that translation for them.

## 3. Perceived Likelihood of Success

A large, desirable outcome that the customer doesn't believe the agency can actually deliver has low perceived value. Confidence-building is not decoration — it's a direct input to the value equation.

### What increases perceived likelihood of success

- Relevant case studies from comparable businesses/segments (see [05_CUSTOMER_VALUE_BY_SEGMENT.md](05_CUSTOMER_VALUE_BY_SEGMENT.md) for segment-specific framing)
- Before-and-after examples of real, verifiable work
- Transparent deliverables — the client knows exactly what they're getting, not a vague promise
- Clear timelines with realistic milestones
- Process explanations — showing the client *how* the work happens, not just that it will
- Evidence of previous results (with permission, and honestly framed — see [../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md) on truthful claims)
- Professional, consistent reporting (built on [../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md](../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md))
- Segment/industry specialization — "we know this business type" reduces perceived risk
- Testimonials from real clients
- Guarantees limited strictly to controllable deliverables (e.g. "your Google Business Profile will be fully verified and complete within 10 business days" — never a guaranteed ranking, revenue figure, or follower count)
- Baseline audits that make the starting point concrete and the improvement measurable
- Clearly defined, relevant KPIs agreed before work starts
- Frequent, proactive communication rather than the client having to chase for updates
- Clearly assigned responsibilities (who does what — see the responsibility comparison in [08_PRICING_PRESENTATION.md](08_PRICING_PRESENTATION.md))
- Honest explanations of genuine uncertainty, rather than false confidence — paradoxically, admitting what can't be guaranteed (see [../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md](../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md) Section A's measurement-limitations framework) builds more trust than overpromising, because it signals the agency isn't inflating the rest of its claims either.

### What must never be used to increase perceived likelihood of success

Never guarantee search rankings, revenue outcomes, follower counts, virality, or any other outcome outside the agency's direct control. This is a hard line, not a style preference — see [16. Ethical Requirements](#16-ethical-requirements-cross-reference) below and [../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md). The agency can guarantee its own controllable deliverables (a profile gets verified, a website gets built to spec, a report gets delivered on time) — never the market's response to them.

## 4. Time Delay

Value is inversely proportional to how long a customer waits to see a result. Two agencies delivering an identical eventual outcome are not equally valuable if one gets there in half the time.

### Levers that genuinely reduce time to value

- Faster onboarding (a streamlined [../operations/06_CLIENT_ONBOARDING.md](../operations/06_CLIENT_ONBOARDING.md) process)
- Prebuilt, segment-appropriate website structures and templates (built from the patterns already established across [../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md](../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md))
- Ready-to-use industry-specific content and copy frameworks
- Immediate correction of inaccurate business information (a same-week NAP fix per [../services/09_LOCAL_SEO.md](../services/09_LOCAL_SEO.md) is one of the fastest real wins available)
- Rapid Google Business Profile optimization — often the single fastest visible improvement an agency can deliver
- Faster content-approval turnaround (a defined, tight approval cycle rather than an open-ended one)
- Priority scheduling for website development
- Shorter response times to client questions/requests
- Rapid campaign deployment once strategy is agreed
- Automated lead routing (per [../services/20_LEAD_GENERATION.md](../services/20_LEAD_GENERATION.md)) so a captured lead reaches a salesperson in minutes, not days
- Direct WhatsApp integration for both customer contact and client communication
- Faster reporting turnaround
- Priority technical support for issues that affect revenue (a broken form, a down site)

### The honesty boundary

Separate genuine speed improvements — a real process, tooling, or prioritization advantage — from an unrealistic promise. "We can have your Google Business Profile fully optimized within 5 business days" is a genuine, deliverable speed claim if the process actually supports it. "You'll be ranking #1 within a week" is not a time-delay claim at all — it's an outcome guarantee the agency doesn't control, and it belongs in the prohibited category from Section 3, not here.

## 5. Customer Effort and Sacrifice

The denominator's second component is what the customer has to give up — time, coordination burden, technical difficulty, decision fatigue — to receive the outcome. This is the single most controllable lever in the entire equation, and it is the primary driver of the tier structure in [03_OFFER_TIERS.md](03_OFFER_TIERS.md).

### What the agency can remove from the customer's plate

- Writing the website copy (the client answers questions; the agency writes — see [../services/04_WEBSITE_COPYWRITING.md](../services/04_WEBSITE_COPYWRITING.md))
- Taking the photographs and filming video (see [../services/16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md](../services/16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md))
- Uploading products, services, and pricing across platforms
- Creating social-media accounts correctly and securely (see [../services/12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md](../services/12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md))
- Publishing content on schedule
- Responding to reviews (see [../services/11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md](../services/11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md))
- Monitoring performance continuously rather than the client having to remember to check
- Preparing reports in plain language
- Managing advertising accounts and budgets
- Connecting leads directly to WhatsApp or a CRM so nothing needs manual re-entry
- Only interrupting the client when a real decision is actually needed — not for routine execution
- Providing a single point of contact instead of the client having to coordinate multiple freelancers/vendors
- Handling technical maintenance (updates, backups, security — see [../services/05_DOMAIN_HOSTING_AND_MAINTENANCE.md](../services/05_DOMAIN_HOSTING_AND_MAINTENANCE.md))

### Why removing effort commands a real premium

A business owner's own time has a real, if unstated, cost — often the highest-value hour in the business, since it's the hour that could otherwise go to serving customers, managing staff, or sourcing product. A service that removes coordination burden, technical difficulty, and decision fatigue is not a "nice to have" layered on top of the same deliverables — it is a materially different product, because it returns the owner's scarcest resource (attention) back to them. This is the entire commercial logic behind the premium tier in [03_OFFER_TIERS.md](03_OFFER_TIERS.md) and the premium-offer design principles in [07_PREMIUM_OFFER_DESIGN.md](07_PREMIUM_OFFER_DESIGN.md): the deliverables can be similar across tiers; what changes most is who carries the effort, the coordination, and the risk.

## 6. Customer Economic Value by Segment

This section applies the value equation's numerator — desired outcome — to real Ethiopian business segments, using the value-driver framework below rather than invented revenue figures. Full detail, including recommended tiers and pricing methods per segment, lives in [05_CUSTOMER_VALUE_BY_SEGMENT.md](05_CUSTOMER_VALUE_BY_SEGMENT.md); this table is the summary reference.

### Value-driver framework

For every segment, the following factors determine how much economic value the agency's work is likely to create, and are rated Low/Medium/High/Very High rather than given a specific currency figure, consistent with the illustrative-scoring discipline in [../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md](../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md):

- Approximate relative value of one additional customer to this business
- Purchase frequency / repeat potential
- Customer lifetime value
- Gross-margin potential (a service business with high margin can afford to pay more per lead than a low-margin retailer)
- Importance of repeat business to the model
- Importance of local search and Maps visibility
- Importance of reviews as a trust signal
- Importance of visual content
- Importance of trust/credibility in the buying decision
- Urgency of customer demand (how time-sensitive is a typical inquiry)
- Roughly how many additional customers would be needed to justify a given marketing fee
- Whether results can actually be measured (see [../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md](../services/24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md) for what's measurable per channel)
- Whether the business model can support a recurring retainer, or is better suited to project-based work

## 16. Ethical Requirements (cross-reference)

The full ethical rulebook governing every file in `commercial/` lives in [../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md) once written, and is summarized wherever relevant throughout this folder. The non-negotiable rules that apply directly to value and pricing communication:

- Never guarantee rankings, revenue, virality, or any outcome outside the agency's direct control.
- Never use fake discounts, false scarcity, inflated reference prices, hidden fees, or deliberately unusable decoy packages.
- Every package offered must be genuinely deliverable and independently valuable — a lower tier can be simpler, but it must never be designed to fail on purpose to force an upgrade.
- Never invent a financial return ("this will 3x your revenue") without a genuine, disclosed evidentiary basis.
