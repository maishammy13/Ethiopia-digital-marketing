# Service: Off-Page SEO and Link Building

## A. Service Definition

**What it is.** Builds the site's credibility signals that come from *outside* the site itself — primarily backlinks from other reputable websites, but also brand mentions, legitimate directory listings, guest content, and partnerships that search engines use as trust and authority signals.

**What business problem it solves.** Technical health (06) and on-page optimization (07) alone cannot overcome low domain authority in a competitive search space — a technically perfect page can still be outranked by a page from a more trusted, better-linked domain. Off-page SEO closes that gap over time.

**Why a customer would pay for it.** Earning legitimate backlinks and mentions requires outreach, relationship-building, and content-creation skills most business owners don't have time for. Done properly (white-hat only), it protects and grows ranking ability for competitive terms rather than risking a manipulative-tactics penalty.

**Business types that benefit most.** Businesses in genuinely competitive search niches, B2B and export-oriented businesses wanting industry authority (see coffee/honey exporters and manufacturers in [../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md](../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md)), and content-rich businesses (media, education, tourism) with genuinely link-worthy material. Lower priority for simple, low-competition local businesses where [09_LOCAL_SEO.md](09_LOCAL_SEO.md) and [10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md](10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md) alone typically deliver most of the achievable value.

**What this service does not include.** Technical SEO (06), on-page content optimization (07), or local-specific citation management tied to NAP (name/address/phone) consistency for Maps visibility — that is covered in 09. This service covers broader link building for domain authority: industry publications, press, guest content, and partnerships, as distinct from local directory citations.

**How it connects to other services.** Depends on a technically healthy site (06) and well-optimized pages (07) to make acquired links worth pursuing. Coordinates with [15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md) for link-worthy content assets. Complements but is distinct from [09_LOCAL_SEO.md](09_LOCAL_SEO.md)'s citation work.

## B. Desired Business Outcomes

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Activity | Backlink audits, outreach, directory/association submissions, content creation for linking, partnership outreach |
| Output | Acquired backlinks, brand mentions, legitimate directory/association listings |
| Outcome | Increased domain authority and trust signals, improving the site's ability to rank for competitive terms |
| Financial impact | Improved ranking for higher-value/competitive keywords that on-page work alone cannot win; occasional direct referral traffic from the links themselves |

## C. Inputs Required From the Client

- Current backlink profile, if known (agency will also audit independently)
- Any past PR/press mentions or media coverage
- Existing partnerships, affiliations, supplier/customer relationships, chamber-of-commerce or association memberships
- Any unique data, research, or content assets the business has that could genuinely attract links
- Awareness of any past manipulative link-building activity or penalty history
- Explicit agreement to a white-hat-only policy (see H)

## D. Detailed Task Breakdown

### 1. Preparation

**Task: Confirm scope and the white-hat-only policy**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Sets a clear ethical boundary before any outreach begins, protecting the client's site from long-term ranking risk |
| Inputs | Client discovery |
| Procedure | 1) Explain to the client why paid/manipulative link schemes are excluded from this service and the risk they carry. 2) Confirm the client's expectations are set around realistic, gradual results rather than instant ranking jumps. |
| Tools | Discovery conversation |
| Deliverable | Documented scope and policy agreement |
| Owner | SEO specialist / account lead |
| Dependency | Onboarding complete |
| Frequency | One-time per client |
| KPI | Agreement documented before outreach begins |
| Quality check | Client explicitly understands and accepts that this is a gradual, evidence-based process |
| Common mistake | Letting a client's impatience push the agency toward risky, fast-but-manipulative tactics |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 2. Research

**Task: Audit the current backlink profile and competitor benchmarks**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Establishes a starting point and reveals what a realistic, comparable target looks like |
| Inputs | Site domain, competitor list from 01 |
| Procedure | 1) Pull the current backlink profile using an available backlink-checking tool. 2) Identify any existing toxic or spammy links needing disavowal. 3) Review the backlink profiles of the competitors identified in strategy for comparison and opportunity ideas. |
| Tools | Google Search Console (basic link data, free), Ahrefs/SEMrush/Moz backlink tools (more complete data, paid) |
| Deliverable | Backlink audit and competitor benchmark |
| Owner | SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time initial audit, repeated quarterly |
| KPI | Audit completed with a documented current link count and quality assessment |
| Quality check | Toxic-link findings cross-checked before recommending any disavowal, since disavowing incorrectly can remove value |
| Common mistake | Recommending disavowal of links that are actually harmless, based on a superficial "spam score" alone |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 3. Strategy

**Task: Build the prioritized link-opportunity list**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Focuses limited outreach effort on the opportunities most likely to succeed and matter |
| Inputs | Backlink audit, competitor benchmark, client's real assets/partnerships |
| Procedure | 1) List realistic opportunity types: relevant business directories, industry/trade associations, chambers of commerce, local press, partner/supplier websites, guest-content opportunities on relevant sites. 2) Prioritize by relevance and realistic likelihood of success, not just domain authority alone. 3) Confirm the plan avoids any low-quality, obviously manipulative link source. |
| Tools | Shared spreadsheet |
| Deliverable | Prioritized link-opportunity list |
| Owner | SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 2.1 |
| Frequency | One-time, revisited quarterly |
| KPI | List includes only relevant, reputable sources |
| Quality check | Each opportunity reviewed for topical relevance to the client's business, not chosen for authority score alone |
| Common mistake | Pursuing high-authority but topically irrelevant sites just for the metric, when relevance matters as much as authority |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 4. Setup

**Task: Set up outreach and link-tracking systems**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Off-page work happens over many small interactions over time — without tracking, effort and results get lost |
| Inputs | Opportunity list |
| Procedure | 1) Build a tracking sheet logging each target, outreach date, response, and outcome. 2) Set up ongoing backlink monitoring to catch new and lost links automatically. |
| Tools | Shared spreadsheet or CRM, backlink-monitoring tool |
| Deliverable | Active outreach-tracking and backlink-monitoring setup |
| Owner | SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 3.1 |
| Frequency | One-time setup |
| KPI | Tracking system active before outreach begins |
| Quality check | Monitoring confirmed to actually detect a known existing link as a test |
| Common mistake | Running outreach without a tracking system, losing visibility into who has been contacted and who has responded |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 5. Production

|---|---|
| Purpose | This is where planned opportunities become actual links and mentions |

| Tools | Email, tracking sheet |

## H. Risks and Common Failure Modes

- **Technical risk:** incorrectly implemented disavow files can remove beneficial links rather than only harmful ones.
- **Platform risk:** search-engine policies on manipulative link schemes (paid links, link farms, excessive reciprocal linking) carry real penalty risk — this service commits to white-hat methods only, explicitly excluding these tactics.
- **Client-related risk:** a client pushing for faster results through purchased or low-quality bulk links — decline and explain the long-term risk clearly (see Task 1.1).
- **Data and access risk:** relying on incomplete free-tier backlink data can miss a meaningful portion of the real link landscape — clearly label data completeness limitations when reporting.
- **Reputation risk:** spammy or irrelevant outreach can damage the client's and agency's reputation with the publishers being contacted.
- **Security risk:** not typically applicable directly to this service.
- **Legal or compliance risk:** any paid placement or sponsored content must be properly disclosed per platform and, where applicable, advertising-standards requirements.
- **Measurement and attribution risk:** ranking changes have many contributing causes; do not claim sole credit for a ranking improvement following a single acquired link.
