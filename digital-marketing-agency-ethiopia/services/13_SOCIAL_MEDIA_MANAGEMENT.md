# Service: Social Media Management

## A. Service Definition

**What it is.** The ongoing operational management of a business's social media accounts: scheduling and publishing content (produced under [15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md), per the plan from [14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md](14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md)), community management (responding to direct messages and comments), monitoring performance, and flagging strong content for paid amplification.

**What business problem it solves.** Accounts that are correctly set up (12) but posted to inconsistently, or whose messages and comments go unanswered, look neglected and quietly lose potential customers who reached out and got no response.

**Why a customer would pay for it.** Consistent posting and responsive community management require daily discipline most business owners don't have time for. Done well, social media becomes a real, trackable inquiry channel rather than a passive brochure nobody visits.

**Business types that benefit most.** Any business where social platforms are a meaningful discovery or engagement channel — the "High"/"Very High" social-media-importance rows in [../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md](../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md) (restaurants, cafés, salons, retail, gyms, event venues, and most other visually strong consumer segments).

**What this does not include.** Initial account creation/setup ([12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md](12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md)), content-pillar and calendar strategy ([14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md](14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md)), actual content creation ([15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md)), or paid advertising campaign management (17/18/19 — this service identifies organic content worth boosting and hands it to those services, it does not run the ad campaigns itself).

**How it connects to other services.** Consumes accounts from 12, the calendar/pillars from 14, and finished assets from 15. Feeds candidates for paid amplification into [17_META_ADVERTISING.md](17_META_ADVERTISING.md)/[19_TIKTOK_ADVERTISING.md](19_TIKTOK_ADVERTISING.md). Routes captured leads into [23_CRM_AND_MARKETING_AUTOMATION.md](23_CRM_AND_MARKETING_AUTOMATION.md) and reports performance through [24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md](24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md).

## B. Desired Business Outcomes

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Activity | Scheduling and publishing posts, responding to DMs/comments, monitoring analytics, flagging boost-worthy content |
| Output | Posts published on schedule, a response log, a monthly performance report |
| Outcome | A consistent, active presence with responsive community management that converts genuine interest into real inquiries |
| Financial impact | Inbound messages and comments converted into leads/sales; avoided lost business from unanswered inquiries |

## C. Inputs Required From the Client

- Live, correctly configured accounts from 12
- The content calendar and pillars from 14
- Finished content assets from 15
- Response-time expectations and an escalation contact for sensitive or complaint-related messages
- Accurate answers to common customer questions, for consistent response handling
- Confirmation of the brand voice guide from [04_WEBSITE_COPYWRITING.md](04_WEBSITE_COPYWRITING.md)/[25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md](25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md)

## D. Detailed Task Breakdown

### 1. Preparation

**Task: Confirm handoff from account setup and the content pipeline**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Management cannot run without live accounts, a calendar, and a content supply already in place |
| Inputs | 12's completed setup, 14's calendar, 15's asset pipeline |
| Procedure | 1) Confirm access to every account in scope. 2) Confirm the current content calendar and upcoming asset delivery schedule. 3) Flag any gap (e.g. no calendar yet) before committing to a publishing start date. |
| Tools | Access checklist, shared calendar |
| Deliverable | Confirmed management-readiness checklist |
| Owner | Social media manager |
| Dependency | 12, Task 8.1; 14 in place |
| Frequency | One-time per client |
| KPI | Checklist completed before the first scheduled post |
| Quality check | Every account in scope has confirmed, working access |
| Common mistake | Committing to a posting schedule before the content pipeline from 14/15 can actually keep up with it |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

**Task: Define response-time targets and escalation rules**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Sets a clear standard for community management and identifies what needs to go to the client directly rather than being handled generically |
| Inputs | Client input on urgency expectations |
| Procedure | 1) Agree a response-time target for DMs and comments (e.g. within business hours same-day). 2) Define what counts as routine (a common question, answerable from FAQ) versus what must escalate immediately (a complaint, a sensitive question, a media inquiry). 3) Document the escalation contact. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Documented response-time targets and escalation rules |
| Owner | Social media manager, account lead |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time, revisited if the client's team changes |
| KPI | Rules documented and shared before management begins |
| Quality check | Escalation rules reviewed against the same service-recovery logic in [11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md](11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md) Task 3.1 for consistency |
| Common mistake | Treating every message as routine and generically answerable, missing a real complaint or sales opportunity requiring a human, specific response |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 2. Research

**Task: Review current platform best practices and format opportunities**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Platform algorithms and favored formats (e.g. short-form video) shift over time; management should adapt rather than run on outdated assumptions |
| Inputs | Platform's own creator/business resources |
| Procedure | 1) Periodically review each platform's current guidance on formats and posting behavior it favors. 2) Note any relevant shift and flag it for the content strategy in 14. |
| Tools | Platform business/creator resource centers |
| Deliverable | Current best-practice notes |
| Owner | Social media manager |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | Quarterly |
| KPI | Notes updated at least once per quarter |
| Quality check | Notes sourced from the platform's own official guidance, not assumption or outdated advice |
| Common mistake | Continuing a format or posting habit long after the platform has clearly shifted its favored content type |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 3. Strategy

| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Posting schedule and community-management protocol |
| Owner | Social media manager |
| Dependency | Task 1.2 |
| Frequency | One-time, revisited quarterly |
| KPI | Every planned post has a specific scheduled time |
| Quality check | Schedule reviewed against realistic staffing capacity, not aspirational volume |
| Common mistake | Committing to a posting frequency that isn't sustainable once the initial launch enthusiasm fades |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

## H. Risks and Common Failure Modes

- **Technical risk:** a scheduling-tool malfunction causing a missed or malformed post.
- **Platform risk:** algorithm and feature changes shifting what content performs, requiring the format/cadence adjustments in Task 10.2.
- **Client-related risk:** the client's content pipeline (14/15) falling behind, leaving nothing ready to schedule.
- **Data and access risk:** losing scheduling-tool or platform access if credentials aren't properly documented (see [../operations/14_SECURITY_AND_ACCESS_MANAGEMENT.md](../operations/14_SECURITY_AND_ACCESS_MANAGEMENT.md)).
- **Reputation risk:** an unanswered DM or comment, or a mishandled complaint response, is visible and can spread — treat community management with the same seriousness as 11's review-response guidance.
- **Security risk:** scheduling-tool and platform access should follow the same role-based, minimum-necessary access principle as [12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md](12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md).
- **Legal or compliance risk:** community-management responses for ⚠️-flagged sectors must respect the same claim restrictions as copywriting and reviews.
- **Measurement and attribution risk:** engagement and follower growth reflect content quality and platform algorithm behavior as much as management discipline — report honestly rather than claiming full credit for organic reach shifts outside the agency's control.

## I. Standard Operating Procedure

- [ ] Access and content pipeline from 12/14/15 confirmed before committing to a start date
- [ ] Response-time targets and escalation rules documented and shared
- [ ] Posting schedule and community-management protocol defined against realistic capacity
- [ ] Scheduling, monitoring, and lead-routing tools set up and tested
- [ ] Every asset paired with a finalized, on-brand caption before its scheduled time
- [ ] Posts published on schedule and spot-checked live after publishing
- [ ] First full cycle completed successfully before formal activation confirmation
- [ ] DMs and comments monitored and responded to within the agreed target every period
- [ ] Engagement, reach, and follower trends reviewed weekly and monthly
- [ ] High-performing content flagged for paid amplification where in scope
- [ ] Cadence/format adjustments proposed based on evidence, not single-post reactions
- [ ] Monthly report delivered connecting activity to real leads/outcomes
- [ ] FAQ answers and escalation rules kept current, reviewed quarterly
