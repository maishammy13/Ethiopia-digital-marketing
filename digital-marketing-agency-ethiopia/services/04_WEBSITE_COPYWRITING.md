# Service: Website Copywriting

## A. Service Definition

**What it is.** Writing the actual words that appear on the website — headlines, service/product descriptions, the about page, FAQ answers, calls-to-action, and meta titles/descriptions — grounded in the personas, keyword/intent research, and conversion goals from [01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md](01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md).

**What business problem it solves.** A well-designed, well-built site can still fail to convert if the words don't clearly explain the offer, address the visitor's real hesitations, or prompt a specific action. Generic, translated-feeling, or vague copy quietly erodes the trust that design and development worked to build.

**Why a customer would pay for it.** Professionally written copy converts better than owner-written or machine-translated text: it reads naturally in the target language, incorporates real search terms without sounding stuffed with keywords, and stays consistent in tone across every page.

**Business types that benefit most.** Any business with meaningful text content, especially where the buying decision depends on explanation and trust rather than a glance at a product photo — healthcare, law, education, professional services, hospitality. Simpler, highly visual sites (e.g. a single-location café) need less copy but still benefit from a clear, well-written core message.

**What this service does not include.** Visual design (02) or development (03) — copywriting produces the words, not the layout or the build. Technical on-page SEO implementation (07) — copywriting incorporates target keywords naturally into the writing, but heading-tag structure, URL formatting, and technical placement belong to on-page SEO. Formal translation into additional languages beyond the primary scope is covered under the translation/localization item in [../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md](../02_SERVICE_CATALOGUE.md) Section G.

**How it connects to other services.** Consumes personas, keyword/intent research, and conversion goals from 01. Feeds directly into [02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md](02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md) Task 6.1 (design is finalized against real copy) and [03_WEBSITE_DEVELOPMENT.md](03_WEBSITE_DEVELOPMENT.md) Task 5.1 (content population). Coordinates closely with [07_ON_PAGE_SEO.md](07_ON_PAGE_SEO.md) for keyword placement and meta content, and with [14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md](14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md)/[25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md](25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md) for a consistent voice across web and social content.

## B. Desired Business Outcomes

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Activity | Interviewing the client, drafting page copy, revising against feedback |
| Output | Final copy for every page: homepage, service/product pages, about, FAQ, calls-to-action, meta titles/descriptions |
| Outcome | Visitors understand the offer, trust the business, and take the intended action; pages are written around real search intent |
| Financial impact | Higher on-site conversion rate; potentially lower cost per lead from paid advertising, since ad traffic lands on copy that actually converts |

## C. Inputs Required From the Client

- Approved Website Strategy Document (personas, keyword/intent report, conversion goals) from 01
- Business description, unique selling points, and preferred tone (formal, warm, technical, etc.)
- Frequently asked customer questions
- Testimonials or reviews the client permits being referenced (with permission — see [11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md](11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md))
- Pricing information or pricing logic, to the extent it will be published
- Required language(s)
- Any regulatory constraints on claims for the client's sector (see ⚠️ flags in [../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md](../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md))
- Existing marketing materials or a brand voice guide, if one exists

## D. Detailed Task Breakdown

### 1. Preparation

**Task: Review strategy inputs and confirm language and tone**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Copy written without grounding in the approved personas and goals tends to default to generic, unfocused language |
| Inputs | Strategy document, client tone preference |
| Procedure | 1) Review personas, keyword/intent report, and conversion goals. 2) Confirm required language(s) and whether content will be written natively in each or translated from one primary version. 3) Confirm tone preference with the client using example phrases, not abstract adjectives alone. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Confirmed language/tone brief |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Strategy sign-off (01, Task 6.1) |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Brief confirmed before drafting begins |
| Quality check | Tone brief includes at least 2–3 concrete example sentences the client agrees represent the desired voice |
| Common mistake | Accepting a vague tone descriptor ("professional but friendly") without concrete examples, leading to mismatched drafts |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 2. Research

**Task: Competitor and comparable-business copy audit**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Identifies what claims and language patterns are standard in this segment, and where the client can differentiate |
| Inputs | Competitor list from strategy |
| Procedure | 1) Read the homepage and one service/product page of each listed competitor. 2) Note common claims, tone, and structure. 3) Identify a differentiation angle the client can credibly claim. |
| Tools | Manual review |
| Deliverable | Short copy-positioning note |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | At least one specific, credible differentiation angle identified |
| Quality check | Differentiation angle is something the business can actually prove, not just a claim |
| Common mistake | Recommending a generic claim ("best quality") that every competitor could equally make |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 3. Strategy

**Task: Define the messaging framework and per-page content outline**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Ensures every page has a clear job (what it needs to say and what action it needs to drive) before drafting starts |
| Inputs | Personas, conversion goals, positioning note |
| Procedure | 1) Define the core message and 3–5 key selling points for the business overall. 2) For each page in the sitemap, outline the specific points it must cover and its primary call-to-action. 3) Confirm the outline against the navigation structure from 02. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Messaging framework and per-page content outline |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Task 2.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Every page in the sitemap has an outline before drafting begins |
| Quality check | Outline reviewed against conversion goals — every page's outline supports its named goal |
| Common mistake | Drafting copy page by page without an overall messaging framework, producing inconsistent claims across pages |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 4. Setup

**Task: Set up the shared copy workflow and style guide**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Keeps terminology, spelling, and tone consistent across every page and across future additions, especially important for bilingual sites |
| Inputs | Tone brief, brand assets |
| Procedure | 1) Create a shared document for drafting and client review with visible comments/tracked changes. 2) Build a short style guide: preferred terms, spelling conventions, and any terms that must stay consistent in both languages if bilingual. |
| Tools | Google Docs or equivalent |
| Deliverable | Copy workflow document and style guide |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Style guide exists before drafting the second page |
| Quality check | Style guide checked against the first draft for consistency |
| Common mistake | Skipping a style guide for a bilingual site, resulting in inconsistent translated terms for the same service across pages |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 5. Production

**Task: Draft homepage and core service/product page copy**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | These are typically the highest-traffic, highest-stakes pages and should be drafted first to validate tone and framework |
| Inputs | Messaging framework, per-page outlines |
| Procedure | 1) Draft the homepage, incorporating the core message and directing visitors to key pages. 2) Draft each core service/product page against its outline, incorporating natural keyword usage from the strategy's keyword/intent report. 3) Write a clear, specific call-to-action on every page. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Draft homepage and service/product page copy |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Task 3.1, 4.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Draft delivered within the agreed timeline |
| Quality check | Self-review against the outline and style guide before sending to the client |
| Common mistake | Writing keyword-stuffed copy that reads unnaturally in an attempt to satisfy SEO — coordinate with [07_ON_PAGE_SEO.md](07_ON_PAGE_SEO.md) for natural integration instead |
| Estimated complexity | High |

**Task: Draft supporting pages (about, FAQ, policy boilerplate, testimonials framing)**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Supporting pages build trust and answer objections that the core pages don't have room for |
| Inputs | Client FAQs, permitted testimonials, positioning note |
| Procedure | 1) Draft the about page around the business's real story and credibility signals. 2) Draft FAQ answers addressing real, common customer questions. 3) Draft short framing copy for a testimonials/reviews section (the reviews themselves are sourced via [11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md](11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md), not invented). 4) Draft any required policy-page boilerplate, flagged for legal review where relevant. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Draft supporting-page copy |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Task 5.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | All supporting pages from the sitemap drafted |
| Quality check | FAQ answers checked against real, client-confirmed questions, not invented ones |
| Common mistake | Writing a generic "About Us" that could describe any business instead of this specific one |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 6. Implementation

**Task: Finalize SEO-aligned meta titles, descriptions, and calls-to-action**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Meta titles and descriptions directly affect click-through from search results; calls-to-action directly affect conversion |
| Inputs | Draft page copy, keyword/intent report |
| Procedure | 1) Write a unique meta title and description per page within platform length limits. 2) Ensure each includes the page's primary keyword naturally. 3) Finalize the wording of every call-to-action button/link, making each specific ("Book a table" rather than "Click here"). |
| Tools | Character-count checker, shared document |
| Deliverable | Meta title/description set, finalized calls-to-action |
| Owner | Copywriter, with SEO specialist review |
| Dependency | Task 5.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Every page has a unique meta title and description within length limits |
| Quality check | No duplicate meta titles/descriptions across pages |
| Common mistake | Leaving default/auto-generated meta descriptions instead of writing them deliberately |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 7. Testing

**Task: Internal review, compliance check, and client review round**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Catches clarity, accuracy, tone, and compliance issues before copy goes into design/development |
| Inputs | Full draft copy set |
| Procedure | 1) Have a second team member proofread for spelling, grammar, and clarity. 2) For ⚠️-flagged segments, check every claim against [../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md). 3) Send to the client for review and fact-checking (the client, not the agency, is the authority on their own prices, credentials, and claims). 4) Incorporate feedback within the agreed revision-round limit. |
| Tools | Grammarly (free tier) or equivalent, shared document with comments |
| Deliverable | Reviewed, client-approved final copy |
| Owner | Second copywriter/editor, then client |
| Dependency | Task 6.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Zero unresolved factual or compliance flags at approval |
| Quality check | Client explicitly confirms accuracy of prices, claims, and credentials in writing |
| Common mistake | Publishing a client-supplied claim (e.g. a specific result or guarantee) without checking it against compliance guidance first |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 8. Launch

**Task: Hand off approved copy for design and development implementation**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Ensures the final, approved copy — not an earlier placeholder version — is what actually gets built and published |
| Inputs | Client-approved final copy |
| Procedure | 1) Deliver the final copy set to the design and development teams in an organized, page-by-page format. 2) Confirm receipt and confirm no placeholder text remains once the copy is implemented (see 02, Task 6.1 and 03, Task 5.1). |
| Tools | Shared document, project-management tool |
| Deliverable | Final copy handoff package |
| Owner | Copywriter, account lead |
| Dependency | Task 7.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Handoff delivered before the design/development implementation deadline |
| Quality check | Development/design team confirms all copy received and matched to the correct page |
| Common mistake | Sending copy in an unorganized format (e.g. one long document with no page labels), causing implementation errors |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 9. Monitoring

**Task: Monitor page-level engagement and conversion post-launch**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Identifies which pages' copy is working and which may need revision, using real visitor behavior rather than guesswork |
| Inputs | Analytics data (see [24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md](24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md)) |
| Procedure | 1) Once sufficient traffic has accumulated, review time-on-page, bounce rate, and conversion rate per page. 2) Flag pages performing notably below others for review. |
| Tools | Google Analytics |
| Deliverable | Page-performance note |
| Owner | Copywriter, with analytics support |
| Dependency | Site live with sufficient traffic |
| Frequency | Quarterly, or sooner if a page shows an obvious problem |
| KPI | Underperforming pages identified with a specific, named metric |
| Quality check | Findings checked against sample size — avoid reacting to a handful of visits |
| Common mistake | Rewriting a page based on one week of data before enough visits have accumulated to draw a reliable conclusion |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 10. Optimization

**Task: Revise underperforming copy based on evidence**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Copy is not "finished" at launch — it should improve as real data becomes available, coordinated with [21_CONVERSION_RATE_OPTIMIZATION.md](21_CONVERSION_RATE_OPTIMIZATION.md) |
| Inputs | Page-performance note |
| Procedure | 1) For a flagged page, form a specific hypothesis about why it underperforms (unclear offer, weak call-to-action, mismatched keyword intent). 2) Rewrite the specific weak element. 3) Monitor whether the change improves the metric. |
| Tools | Shared document, analytics |
| Deliverable | Revised copy and before/after comparison |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Task 9.1 |
| Frequency | As needed, reviewed quarterly |
| KPI | Measurable improvement in the targeted metric after the change |
| Quality check | Only one meaningful variable changed at a time where practical, so the cause of any improvement is identifiable |
| Common mistake | Changing multiple elements of a page at once, making it impossible to tell which change caused a result |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 11. Reporting

**Task: Summarize copy decisions and rationale for the client**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Helps the client understand and maintain the copy's intent as they request future changes themselves |
| Inputs | Final copy, messaging framework |
| Procedure | 1) Provide a short plain-language summary of the core message, key selling points, and why the calls-to-action are worded as they are. 2) Include this with the launch handoff and with any Task 10.1 revisions. |
| Tools | Summary document |
| Deliverable | Plain-language copy rationale summary |
| Owner | Copywriter |
| Dependency | Task 8.1 |
| Frequency | At initial delivery and with significant revisions |
| KPI | Client can correctly restate the core message back after review |
| Quality check | Summary avoids unexplained jargon |
| Common mistake | Assuming the client will infer the strategy behind the copy without it being explained |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 12. Maintenance

**Task: Keep copy current as prices, services, and offers change**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Outdated prices or discontinued services published on the site damage trust and can create real customer disputes |
| Inputs | Client updates on business changes |
| Procedure | 1) Establish a simple process for the client to flag business changes (new prices, new services, discontinued offers). 2) Update the relevant copy promptly. 3) Periodically audit the live site copy against the style guide for consistency as pages are added over time. |
| Tools | Shared document, CMS access |
| Deliverable | Currently accurate, consistent site copy |
| Owner | Copywriter or account lead |
| Dependency | Ongoing |
| Frequency | As-needed, with a light quarterly consistency audit |
| KPI | No stale price/service information found during quarterly audit |
| Quality check | Audit checklist run each quarter |
| Common mistake | Letting outdated pricing sit on the live site for months after a client update because there is no defined update process |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

## E. Deliverables

**Initial deliverables**
- Final copy for every page in the sitemap (homepage, service/product pages, about, FAQ, testimonials framing, policy-page boilerplate)
- Meta titles and descriptions for every page
- A short style guide (tone, terminology, bilingual consistency where relevant)
- Plain-language copy rationale summary

**Quarterly deliverables**
- Page-performance review and any resulting copy revisions (Tasks 9.1–10.1)
- Consistency audit against the style guide (Task 12.1)

**Optional add-ons**
- Formal translation into additional languages
- Blog or resource-article writing (coordinate with [14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md](14_CONTENT_STRATEGY.md)/[15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md))
- Case-study or testimonial long-form write-ups

**Monthly deliverables:** none by default — copy work is front-loaded at launch, then maintained as-needed and reviewed quarterly.

## F. KPIs

| KPI | Type | Attribution caution |
|---|---|---|
| Copy delivered within the agreed revision-round limit and timeline | Leading | Directly attributable |
| Every page has a unique meta title/description and specific call-to-action | Leading | Directly attributable |
| Zero unresolved compliance flags at approval | Leading | Directly attributable |

## G. Tools

| Category | Tools | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Google Docs, Grammarly (free tier), Hemingway App (readability check) | Sufficient for the large majority of SME copywriting work |
| Low-cost | Grammarly Premium | Useful for higher-volume or bilingual work needing more thorough checking |

| Low-bandwidth / mobile-first delivery | Plain shareable documents rather than heavy design files; keep review files small enough to open comfortably on a slow connection | Copy review should not require a fast connection to participate in |

## H. Risks and Common Failure Modes

- **Technical risk:** minimal directly, but meta description/title length limits vary by platform and change over time — verify current limits before finalizing.
- **Platform risk:** search engines may rewrite meta titles/descriptions in results if they judge the content insufficiently relevant — write clear, accurate copy to minimize this rather than attempting to game it.
- **Client-related risk:** a client requesting exaggerated or unverifiable claims — flag and redirect to a claim the business can actually support.
- **Data and access risk:** using a customer testimonial or review without explicit permission.
- **Reputation risk:** generic, AI-generated-feeling, or awkwardly translated copy undermines the professional impression the whole website is meant to create.
- **Security risk:** not typically applicable to this service directly.
- **Legal or compliance risk:** ⚠️-flagged segments (healthcare, financial services, education, law) have specific restrictions on claims, guarantees, and before/after language — verify every such claim against [../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md) before publishing.
- **Measurement and attribution risk:** attributing a conversion-rate change entirely to a copy edit when design, traffic source, or seasonality changed at the same time.

## I. Standard Operating Procedure

- [ ] Strategy inputs reviewed; language and tone brief confirmed with concrete examples
- [ ] Competitor copy audit completed; a credible differentiation angle identified
- [ ] Messaging framework and per-page content outline defined
- [ ] Style guide and shared copy workflow set up
- [ ] Homepage and core service/product page copy drafted against outlines
- [ ] Supporting-page copy (about, FAQ, testimonials framing, policy boilerplate) drafted
- [ ] Meta titles, descriptions, and calls-to-action finalized within platform limits
- [ ] Internal proofread and compliance check completed
- [ ] Client review round completed; all facts/claims explicitly confirmed by the client
- [ ] Final copy handed off to design/development, page-by-page and organized
- [ ] Page-level performance monitored post-launch and reviewed quarterly
- [ ] Underperforming copy revised based on evidence, one variable at a time
- [ ] Quarterly consistency audit run against the style guide
