# Service: Local SEO

## A. Service Definition

**What it is.** The discipline of making a business visible in local, "near me," and city/neighborhood-specific search and map results, across the web — not limited to any single platform. Covers name/address/phone (NAP) consistency across directories, citation building, local landing pages on the website, local structured data, local keyword targeting, and cross-platform local rank tracking.

**What business problem it solves.** Most local customers search with local intent ("best [service] in [neighborhood]," "[service] near me") and choose from what search engines and maps surface immediately. Inconsistent business information across the web — a different phone number on one directory, an old address on another — confuses both search engines and customers and directly suppresses local ranking, even if the business's own website is excellent.

**Why a customer would pay for it.** Local search is often the single highest-intent, highest-conversion traffic source a small local business gets — someone searching "[service] near me" is typically ready to act now. Most SME owners have never audited their information consistency or deliberately built local citations, leaving real, low-cost ranking gains unclaimed.

**Business types that benefit most.** Proximity-driven, walk-in or call-in businesses — restaurants, salons, clinics, home services, auto repair, laundromats — see the "Very High" local-discovery and Maps-dependence ratings across most consumer-facing rows in [../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md](../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md). Multi-location businesses benefit especially from the location-landing-page work in this service.

**What this does not include.** Deep, ongoing management of the Google Business Profile listing itself — that is [10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md](10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md). This service treats the Google Business Profile as one of many citation sources requiring NAP consistency, not as its full management. It also does not include sitewide technical SEO (06), general on-page SEO of non-location pages (07), or the review-generation/response process itself ([11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md](11_REVIEW_AND_REPUTATION_MANAGEMENT.md)) — though reviews are referenced here as a known local-ranking factor.

**How it connects to other services.** Depends on a technically healthy site (06) so location pages are crawlable, and on-page optimization discipline (07) for those pages. Works as one of three tightly linked local-visibility pillars alongside 10 (Google Business Profile) and 11 (reviews) — all three should be sold and reasoned about together for a proximity-driven business, even when only one is initially in scope. Feeds call, direction-request, and local-traffic data into [24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md](24_ANALYTICS_TRACKING_AND_REPORTING.md).

## B. Desired Business Outcomes

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Activity | NAP consistency audit, citation building, location-landing-page creation, local schema markup, local keyword research, local rank tracking |
| Output | Consistent business information across directories, built citations, local landing pages, local structured data, a local keyword map, a local rank-tracking record |
| Outcome | Higher visibility in local/map search results for nearby, high-intent customers |
| Financial impact | More calls, direction requests, and store visits directly traceable to local search — typically among the most cost-efficient traffic a local business receives |

## C. Inputs Required From the Client

- Exact, final business name, address, telephone number, and WhatsApp number, in the precise form to be used everywhere (see Task 1.1)
- Service area (single location, multiple locations, or a defined radius/set of neighborhoods)
- Opening hours, including special/seasonal hours
- Full list of locations if multi-location
- Awareness of existing directory listings, if known (agency will also audit independently)
- Target local keywords/neighborhoods, informed by the keyword/intent report from [01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md](01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md)
- Categories/services to be listed consistently across platforms

## D. Detailed Task Breakdown

### 1. Preparation

**Task: Standardize the exact NAP format**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Every citation and listing must use an identical business name, address, and phone format — even small formatting differences (e.g. "St." vs. "Street," a missing suite number) can undermine consistency signals |
| Inputs | Client's official business details |
| Procedure | 1) Confirm the exact legal/trading business name to be used. 2) Confirm the exact address format, including any building/floor/area detail. 3) Confirm the exact phone number format (with consistent country-code formatting). 4) Document this as the single source of truth. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Standardized NAP reference document |
| Owner | Local SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Onboarding complete |
| Frequency | One-time per client (updated whenever business details change — see Task 12.1) |
| KPI | NAP reference approved by the client before any citation work begins |
| Quality check | Reference double-checked against the client's actual signage/legal documents, not assumed |
| Common mistake | Using a slightly different address format across different listings, weakening consistency signals without anyone noticing until an audit |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 2. Research

**Task: Audit current NAP consistency and existing citations**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Reveals where the business's information is currently wrong, outdated, inconsistent, or duplicated before any new work is added on top |
| Inputs | Standardized NAP reference |
| Procedure | 1) Search for the business across major directories, map platforms, and any industry-specific listing sites. 2) Record every existing listing found and compare against the standardized NAP. 3) Flag inconsistencies, outdated information, and any duplicate listings. |
| Tools | Manual search, Google Maps, Google Search |
| Deliverable | NAP consistency audit report |
| Owner | Local SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time initial audit, repeated quarterly |
| KPI | All findable existing listings documented |
| Quality check | Audit checked against multiple search variations (business name alone, name + city, name + category) to avoid missing listings |
| Common mistake | Missing an old, outdated listing from a previous address or phone number that is still live and actively hurting consistency |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

**Task: Local keyword research and competitor map-pack audit**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Local landing pages and citation categories should be built around real local search terms and informed by what's currently winning local visibility |
| Inputs | Keyword/intent report from 01, competitor list |
| Procedure | 1) Identify local-intent keyword variations (neighborhood names, "near me" equivalents, city-specific terms). 2) Search key local terms and review which competitors appear in the map pack/local results. 3) Note what those competitors have in common (reviews, categories, completeness of listing). |
| Tools | Google Search, Google Maps |
| Deliverable | Local keyword list and map-pack competitor notes |
| Owner | Local SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time initial pass, revisited quarterly |
| KPI | At least the top 5–10 relevant local search terms documented |
| Quality check | Terms checked for genuine local search behavior, not just assumed |
| Common mistake | Assuming national keyword volume data applies evenly to hyper-local search terms, which often have limited or unreliable volume data (see the same caution in 01) |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 3. Strategy

**Task: Build the citation-building target list**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Focuses citation-building effort on directories that are relevant, reputable, and likely to actually help, not every directory that exists |

| Tools | Shared spreadsheet |
| Deliverable | Prioritized citation target list |
| Owner | Local SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 2.1 |
| Frequency | One-time, revisited quarterly |
| KPI | Target list includes only relevant, reputable sources |

**Task: Plan location-specific landing pages**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | For a multi-location or multi-area business, a single generic page cannot rank well for every location's local searches — each significant location or service area needs its own page |
| Inputs | List of locations/service areas, local keyword research |
| Procedure | 1) Confirm which locations or service areas justify a dedicated page (a genuine physical location or clearly defined service area, not an artificial page created only for SEO). 2) Outline each page's required content (local address, local phone, local hours if different, area-specific information, map embed). |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Location-landing-page plan |
| Owner | Local SEO specialist, coordinating with 01/02 for sites already in strategy/design |
| Dependency | Task 3.1 |
| Frequency | One-time, updated when locations change |
| KPI | Every genuine location/service area has a planned page |
| Quality check | Each planned page has a legitimate reason to exist, not created purely to target a keyword with no real substance |
| Common mistake | Creating near-duplicate location pages with only the city name changed and no genuinely unique local content, which search engines may treat as low-value or duplicate |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

## H. Risks and Common Failure Modes

- **Technical risk:** incorrect structured data or a broken location page undermines the very consistency this service is meant to build.
- **Platform risk:** individual directories change their submission processes or ownership-verification requirements over time — expect some rework per platform.
- **Client-related risk:** a client changing business details (moving location, changing phone number) without notifying the agency, silently reintroducing inconsistency.
- **Data and access risk:** claiming or correcting a listing on a directory the client doesn't actually have verified ownership/access to.
- **Reputation risk:** thin, near-duplicate location pages built purely for keyword targeting can read as low-effort and may be treated as low-value or duplicate content by search engines.
- **Security risk:** directory account credentials should follow the same access-control discipline as other accounts — see [../operations/14_SECURITY_AND_ACCESS_MANAGEMENT.md](../operations/14_SECURITY_AND_ACCESS_MANAGEMENT.md).
- **Legal or compliance risk:** for ⚠️-flagged sectors, ensure directory listing categories and descriptions don't imply unverified claims (e.g. a category implying a medical specialty the practitioner doesn't hold).
- **Measurement and attribution risk:** local ranking is influenced by Google Business Profile quality and review signals as much as by this service's own work — report local visibility as a shared outcome across the three pillars (09, 10, 11), not a single service's sole achievement.

## I. Standard Operating Procedure

- [ ] Standardized NAP reference approved by the client before any citation work begins
- [ ] Existing NAP consistency audited across all findable directories/listings
- [ ] Local keyword research and competitor map-pack audit completed
- [ ] Citation target list and location-landing-page plan built
- [ ] Local rank tracking and citation-tracking systems set up with a recorded baseline
- [ ] Location-specific landing pages written with genuinely unique content and validated local structured data
- [ ] Existing inconsistent citations corrected; new relevant citations submitted
- [ ] All corrections and new listings validated live, not assumed from submission alone
- [ ] Location pages included in the sitemap and indexing requested
- [ ] Local rankings and location-driven actions (calls, direction requests, clicks) monitored monthly
- [ ] Underperforming pages refined and new NAP inconsistencies fixed immediately upon discovery
- [ ] Monthly report connects activity to real business outcomes
- [ ] Full NAP/citation audit repeated quarterly; business-detail changes propagated everywhere without delay
