# Service: Website Strategy

## A. Service Definition

**What it is.** The discovery and planning process that happens before any design or code work: defining what the website must achieve, who it is for, what content and functions it needs, how it will be found, and how success will be measured. The output is a written strategy document and site map that [02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md](02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md) and [03_WEBSITE_DEVELOPMENT.md](03_WEBSITE_DEVELOPMENT.md) are built against.

**What business problem it solves.** Without this step, websites get built around what looks good rather than what the target customer actually searches for, needs to see, or needs to do to convert. This produces expensive rework, sites that rank for nothing, and sites that get traffic but no inquiries.

**Why a customer would pay for it.** It reduces the risk of paying for a website twice — once to build it wrong, once to fix it. It also ensures the more expensive downstream services (design, development, copywriting, SEO, ads) are all pointed at the same, correctly-researched target.

**Business types that benefit most.** Any business making a meaningful investment in a new site or a full redesign, especially research-driven or higher-value-decision segments (hotels, clinics, law firms, schools, real estate, B2B exporters) where the website plays a real role in the buying decision — see [../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md](../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md). A business needing only a single-page contact/location presence may not need a full strategy engagement — see §E "Optional add-ons vs. required."

**What this service does not include.** Visual design (02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md), actual coding or CMS build (03_WEBSITE_DEVELOPMENT.md), final on-page copy (04_WEBSITE_COPYWRITING.md), domain/hosting setup (05_DOMAIN_HOSTING_AND_MAINTENANCE.md), or execution of SEO (06/07/08/09). This service defines requirements for all of those; it does not produce their final output.

**How it connects to other services.** Directly feeds 02, 03, and 04. Keyword and search-intent research here feeds [06_TECHNICAL_SEO.md](06_TECHNICAL_SEO.md) and [07_ON_PAGE_SEO.md](07_ON_PAGE_SEO.md). Conversion-goal definition here feeds [20_LEAD_GENERATION.md](20_LEAD_GENERATION.md) and [21_CONVERSION_RATE_OPTIMIZATION.md](21_CONVERSION_RATE_OPTIMIZATION.md).

## B. Desired Business Outcomes

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Activity | Conducting stakeholder interviews, competitor audits, and keyword research |
| Output | Website Strategy Document, sitemap, keyword/search-intent report, persona summary, conversion-goal definition |
| Outcome | A website built once, correctly, that ranks for terms real customers use and is structured around how they actually decide |
| Financial impact | Reduced rework/redesign cost; faster time from launch to first qualified lead; higher downstream conversion rate because the site targets real intent rather than guesses |

## C. Inputs Required From the Client

- Business name, address, phone/WhatsApp number, email
- Business objectives for the website (e.g. more bookings, more calls, showcase a portfolio, sell online)
- Target customers and the languages they use
- Geographic service area
- Products, services, and prices (or pricing logic if prices vary)
- Existing website and analytics access, if this is a redesign
- Known competitors, if any (agency will also research independently — see D.2)
- Brand assets, if they already exist (logo, colors) — otherwise deferred to [25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md](25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md)
- Availability of a decision-maker for a discovery interview/workshop
- Any legal or regulatory constraints on the business's advertising or claims (see ⚠️ flags in [../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md](../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md))

### 2. Research

**Task: Competitor and market research**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Identifies what the client is being compared against and what a credible site in this segment needs to include |
| Inputs | Client-named competitors, independent search using target keywords |
| Procedure | 1) Search the client's core services/products as a customer would. 2) Record the top 5–10 results (organic and Maps). 3) Audit each for site structure, content, calls-to-action, review volume, and obvious gaps. 4) Summarize patterns and opportunities. |
| Tools | Google Search, Google Maps, manual site review; free tools like Google's Mobile-Friendly Test |
| Deliverable | Competitor summary section of the strategy document |
| Owner | Strategist / SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project (refreshed quarterly, see §9 Monitoring) |
| KPI | At least 5 relevant competitors documented |
| Quality check | Competitors are genuinely comparable (same segment/geography), not arbitrary |
| Common mistake | Comparing against large international brands that are not realistic local competitors |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

**Task: Target-customer and customer-journey research**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Design and content decisions should follow how the real customer discovers, evaluates, and decides — not assumptions |
| Inputs | Client input on typical customers, any existing customer data/reviews |

| Tools | Shared document, existing customer reviews as evidence |
| Deliverable | Persona summary and journey map section |
| Owner | Strategist |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per project |
| KPI | Personas grounded in at least one real evidence source (client input, reviews, or comparable-segment data), not invented |
| Quality check | Each persona ties to a specific website requirement (e.g. "needs pricing visible" → pricing page) |
| Common mistake | Writing generic personas that don't change any actual design or content decision |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

**Task: Keyword and search-intent research**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Ensures pages are built around terms real customers search, not internal company language |
| Inputs | Client's products/services list, target geography, target languages |
| Procedure | 1) List core services/products in plain customer language. 2) Use keyword tools to find related search terms and estimate relative demand. 3) Classify each term by intent (informational, navigational, transactional, local). 4) Flag terms with limited or unreliable local-language search-volume data rather than guessing a number. |
| Tools | Google Keyword Planner (free with an ads account), Google Trends (free), Google Search autosuggest, Google Search Console (post-launch validation) |
| Deliverable | Keyword and search-intent report |
| Owner | SEO specialist |
| Dependency | Task 2.2 |
| Frequency | One-time per project (refreshed quarterly) |
| KPI | Core service pages each mapped to at least one primary keyword/intent |
| Quality check | No keyword-volume figure presented to the client without a named source or an explicit "estimate, low local data confidence" label |
| Common mistake | Treating US/global keyword-volume tool estimates as reliable for Amharic-language or hyper-local Ethiopian search terms without caveats |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

## H. Risks and Common Failure Modes

- **Technical risk:** local-language (Amharic) and hyper-local keyword-volume data is often sparse or unreliable in mainstream keyword tools — treat volume estimates as directional, not precise, and say so to the client.
- **Platform risk:** recommending a platform in Task 4.1 without confirming it supports required integrations (WhatsApp, payments, booking) available in Ethiopia at delivery time.
- **Client-related risk:** an unavailable or disengaged decision-maker during discovery produces a strategy built on assumptions rather than confirmed input — do not proceed to sign-off (Task 6.1) without real client engagement.
- **Data and access risk:** for redesigns, delayed access to existing analytics/Search Console data can stall competitor and keyword validation — request access during onboarding, not at the start of research.
- **Reputation risk:** recommending an over-scoped site (features the business doesn't need) damages trust when the client later realizes the budget was misallocated.
- **Measurement and attribution risk:** claiming credit for post-launch traffic or conversion gains without acknowledging the contribution of design, copywriting, and SEO execution overstates this service's isolated impact.
- **Legal/compliance risk:** competitor research and content-scope decisions for ⚠️-flagged segments (healthcare, financial services, law) must respect the advertising-claim restrictions noted in [../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md](../market-mapping/01_ETHIOPIA_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_MAP.md) and [../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md](../operations/15_LEGAL_COMPLIANCE_AND_RISK.md).
