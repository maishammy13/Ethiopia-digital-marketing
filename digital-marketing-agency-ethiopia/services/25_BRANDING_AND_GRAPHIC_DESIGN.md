# Service: Branding and Graphic Design

## A. Service Definition

**What it is.** Creates and maintains the foundational visual identity — logo, color palette, typography, brand voice/tone guidance, and core design templates — that every other visual and content-producing service in this system builds on.

**What business problem it solves.** Without a defined identity, every deliverable across services ends up looking inconsistent: different colors on the website versus Instagram versus a printed flyer, a logo that looks different in every context, no consistent tone. This undermines the professionalism and recognition every other service is trying to build. Without a documented system, every new designer or freelancer working on the account reinvents the visual approach from scratch.

**Why a customer would pay for it.** A professional, cohesive identity builds trust and recognition far faster than an inconsistent, ad hoc visual presence. Establishing it once, properly, saves rework across every other visual service permanently.

**Business types that benefit most.** Any new business, or any business formalizing or refreshing its identity. This service ideally happens early — before or alongside [01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md](01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md)/[12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md](12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md) — since so much downstream work depends on it.

**What this does not include.** Photography/video production itself ([16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md](16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md), though this service's guidelines inform its visual style). Website design execution ([02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md](02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md) applies these assets, it doesn't create them). Individual content-piece design ([15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md) uses the templates and assets this service creates, on an ongoing basis, rather than designing a brand system itself).

**How it connects to other services.** Feeds nearly every visual/content service in the catalogue: [02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md](02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md)/[03_WEBSITE_DEVELOPMENT.md](03_WEBSITE_DEVELOPMENT.md), [10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md](10_GOOGLE_BUSINESS_PROFILE.md)'s visual assets, [12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md](12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md)/[13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md](13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md)/[15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md), [16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md](16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md)'s visual direction, and the creative used in [17_META_ADVERTISING.md](17_META_ADVERTISING.md)/[18_GOOGLE_ADVERTISING.md](18_GOOGLE_ADVERTISING.md)/[19_TIKTOK_ADVERTISING.md](19_TIKTOK_ADVERTISING.md).

## B. Desired Business Outcomes

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Activity | Brand discovery, logo design, color and typography selection, brand-guideline documentation, template creation |
| Output | Logo files in multiple formats, a brand style guide, core templates (social, print, presentation) |
| Outcome | A consistent, recognizable, professional visual identity applied correctly across every channel |
| Financial impact | Faster, cheaper production of every subsequent visual deliverable, since the style doesn't need reinventing each time; improved trust and recognition from genuine consistency |

## C. Inputs Required From the Client

- Business name, story, and values
- Target customer/persona information (from 01/market-mapping, if available)
- Any existing brand assets, even informal ones (an old logo, a color the owner already associates with the business), to evaluate what to keep versus redesign
- Style preferences and anything to explicitly avoid
- Languages required on brand assets (e.g. Amharic and English text rendering together)
- Realistic budget and timeline for the identity project

## D. Detailed Task Breakdown

### 1. Preparation

**Task: Conduct brand discovery with the client**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | A visual identity should reflect the real business's story and values, not a generic aesthetic choice |
| Inputs | Client interview |
| Procedure | 1) Interview the client about the business's story, values, and personality. 2) Confirm the target customer and how the business wants to be perceived by them. 3) Review any existing informal brand assets for what to keep or discard. |
| Tools | Discovery questionnaire, interview |
| Deliverable | Brand discovery notes |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Onboarding complete |
| Frequency | One-time per client |
| KPI | Discovery notes cover story, values, target customer, and existing-asset assessment |
| Quality check | Notes reviewed for specificity — vague answers ("we want to look professional") should be probed further |
| Common mistake | Skipping discovery and jumping straight to logo concepts based on the designer's own taste rather than the actual business |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 2. Research

**Task: Competitor and category visual audit**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Reveals the visual conventions of the category, so the identity can either fit in credibly or differentiate deliberately, not by accident |
| Inputs | Competitor list |
| Procedure | 1) Review the visual identity (logo, color, style) of competitors and comparable businesses in the category. 2) Note common conventions and identify a genuine differentiation opportunity. |
| Tools | Manual research |
| Deliverable | Competitor visual audit notes |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time |
| KPI | At least a handful of relevant comparables reviewed |
| Quality check | Findings specific, not a vague impression |
| Common mistake | Designing an identity that unintentionally looks like it belongs to a specific existing competitor |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 3. Strategy

**Task: Translate brand personality into a visual and color/typography direction**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Converts the discovery findings into concrete design decisions with a stated rationale, before any logo concepts are drawn |
| Inputs | Discovery notes, competitor audit |
| Procedure | 1) Define the brand's personality in a few clear terms (e.g. warm and traditional, modern and efficient, playful and approachable). 2) Propose a color palette and typography direction matched to that personality and to the target customer. 3) Confirm the direction supports both English and Amharic (or other required-language) text rendering clearly. |
| Tools | Shared document, mood/reference boards |
| Deliverable | Visual direction brief |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 2.1 |
| Frequency | One-time |
| KPI | Direction approved by the client before logo concepting begins |
| Quality check | Every proposed color/typography choice traceable to a stated reason from discovery |
| Common mistake | Choosing colors or fonts based on personal designer preference rather than the brand personality and target-customer fit established in discovery |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 4. Setup

**Task: Set up the design project and asset-library structure**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Establishes an organized foundation the whole identity project — and its long-term use by other services — will be built and stored in |
| Inputs | Visual direction brief |
| Procedure | 1) Create the design project file with a clear naming and folder convention. 2) Set up the eventual asset-library structure that will hold final files, organized for easy use by other services later. |
| Tools | Design software project file, shared cloud storage |
| Deliverable | Organized design project and future asset-library structure |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 3.1 |
| Frequency | One-time |
| KPI | Structure in place before logo concepting begins |
| Quality check | Structure reviewed for whether another team member could navigate it without explanation |
| Common mistake | Working in an unorganized file with no clear structure, making it harder to hand off final assets cleanly later |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 5. Production

**Task: Design logo concepts**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Produces the initial creative options for client review before committing to full development |
| Inputs | Visual direction brief |
| Procedure | 1) Develop a small number of genuinely distinct logo concepts (not minor variations of one idea) reflecting the approved direction. 2) Present concepts in context (e.g. on a sign, a phone screen) rather than as isolated flat art. |
| Tools | Design software (e.g. Adobe Illustrator, Figma) |
| Deliverable | Logo concept presentation |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 4.1 |
| Frequency | One-time |
| KPI | 2–3 genuinely distinct concepts presented |
| Quality check | Each concept checked for legibility at small sizes before presenting |
| Common mistake | Presenting several minor variations of a single idea rather than genuinely distinct concepts, giving the client a false sense of choice |
| Estimated complexity | High |

**Task: Develop the full logo system, color palette, and typography**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | A single logo file isn't enough — real-world use requires a full system of variants and defined supporting elements |
| Inputs | Approved logo concept |
| Procedure | 1) Develop the primary logo plus necessary variants (icon-only/mark, horizontal/stacked lockups, single-color and reversed/white versions). 2) Finalize the color palette with exact color codes (hex/RGB/CMYK for both digital and print use). 3) Finalize primary and secondary typefaces, confirming licensing for business use. |
| Tools | Design software |
| Deliverable | Full logo system, finalized color palette, and typography |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Client's chosen concept from Task 5.1 |
| Frequency | One-time |
| KPI | Every practically needed logo variant produced |
| Quality check | Logo tested at small sizes and in single-color to confirm it still reads clearly |
| Common mistake | Delivering only a single full-color logo file, leaving other services to awkwardly improvise a single-color or small-size version themselves later |
| Estimated complexity | High |

### 6. Implementation

**Task: Apply the identity to core templates**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Demonstrates the identity in practical use and gives other services a ready starting template rather than building from nothing |
| Inputs | Finalized logo, color palette, typography |
| Procedure | 1) Apply the identity to core templates: social media profile/cover images, a presentation/proposal template, and print basics (business card, letterhead) as relevant to the client's needs. |
| Tools | Design software, Canva (for editable ongoing templates) |
| Deliverable | Core brand templates |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 5.2 |
| Frequency | One-time, expanded as new template needs arise (Task 10.1) |
| KPI | Core templates cover the client's actual near-term needs |
| Quality check | Templates checked for genuine reusability (editable text fields, correct dimensions) rather than being one-off static designs |
| Common mistake | Delivering only static, non-editable template examples instead of genuinely reusable, easily editable templates for ongoing use |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 7. Testing

**Task: Test legibility, reproduction, and accessibility**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Confirms the identity actually works in the real, imperfect conditions it will be used in, not just on a polished presentation slide |
| Inputs | Finalized logo and color palette |
| Procedure | 1) Test the logo's legibility at very small sizes (e.g. a social profile icon) and in single-color/grayscale reproduction. 2) Test the color palette's text/background contrast against accessibility minimums, consistent with the standard in [02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md](02_WEBSITE_DESIGN.md) Task 7.1. |
| Tools | WebAIM Contrast Checker, small-size print/screen mockups |
| Deliverable | Verified, real-condition-tested identity |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 5.2 |
| Frequency | One-time |
| KPI | Zero accessibility contrast failures on the core palette combinations |
| Quality check | Tested at actual real-world sizes, not just viewed large on a design screen |
| Common mistake | Approving a logo that looks great large on a monitor but becomes illegible as a small social media profile icon |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 8. Launch

**Task: Present the final identity for approval and deliver final files**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Confirms client sign-off and hands over a complete, usable asset package |
| Inputs | Tested, finalized identity |
| Procedure | 1) Present the final identity system with its rationale. 2) Incorporate any final feedback within the agreed revision-round limit. 3) Deliver final files in every format needed (vector source files, PNG/JPG exports, web-optimized versions) alongside the brand style guide. |
| Tools | Presentation, shared drive |
| Deliverable | Client-approved final identity and delivered asset package |
| Owner | Brand designer, account lead |
| Dependency | Task 7.1 |
| Frequency | One-time |
| KPI | Sign-off obtained within the agreed revision-round limit |
| Quality check | Delivered file package checked against a standard checklist (vector source, all logo variants, color codes, font files/licensing info) before sending |
| Common mistake | Delivering only a flattened image file instead of the editable vector source file, which severely limits future use by other services |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 9. Monitoring

**Task: Spot-check consistent application across live channels**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | An identity is only valuable if it's actually applied consistently once delivered — drift happens easily across many touchpoints and team members |
| Inputs | Live website, social profiles, and other channels |
| Procedure | 1) Periodically review the live website, social accounts, GBP, and any produced content for consistency with the approved identity. 2) Note and flag any drift (wrong color, outdated logo version, inconsistent typography). |
| Tools | Manual review across channels |
| Deliverable | Brand-consistency monitoring notes |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 8.1 |
| Frequency | Quarterly |
| KPI | Drift identified and flagged within the same quarterly cycle it's found |
| Quality check | Checked against the actual style guide, not the reviewer's memory of it |
| Common mistake | Never checking back after initial delivery, allowing inconsistency to accumulate silently across services and time as different people produce content |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 10. Optimization

**Task: Extend the system as new needs arise**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | A brand system should grow to cover new practical needs rather than being treated as fixed and complete forever |
| Inputs | New template/asset requests from other services |
| Procedure | 1) When another service identifies a need for a new template type or asset not yet covered, design it consistent with the existing system. 2) Add it to the asset library. |
| Tools | Design software, asset library |
| Deliverable | Extended brand asset library |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Task 9.1, ongoing requests |
| Frequency | As needed |
| KPI | New requests fulfilled without introducing inconsistency with the existing system |
| Quality check | New assets checked against the style guide before being added to the library |
| Common mistake | Designing a one-off new asset under time pressure without checking it against the established style guide, introducing a small but real inconsistency |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

### 11. Reporting

**Task: Deliver the brand asset handoff summary**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Gives the client and every other service a clear reference for what exists and how to use it |
| Inputs | Final delivered assets |
| Procedure | 1) Summarize what was delivered, where it's stored, and how to request future additions. 2) Confirm every relevant internal team has access to the asset library. |
| Tools | Summary document |
| Deliverable | Brand asset handoff summary |
| Owner | Brand designer, account lead |
| Dependency | Task 8.1 |
| Frequency | One-time at delivery, updated with each Task 10.1 extension |
| KPI | Summary delivered alongside the final asset package |
| Quality check | Confirmed every team likely to need these assets actually has access |
| Common mistake | Delivering the assets only to the account lead without confirming the actual production teams (web, social, ads) have direct access |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 12. Maintenance

**Task: Maintain the organized, versioned asset library**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Keeps the identity system usable and current indefinitely, rather than degrading into scattered, outdated files over time |
| Inputs | Ongoing use and extension of the identity |
| Procedure | 1) Keep the asset library organized as it grows. 2) Retire outdated versions clearly (never leave an old logo version ambiguously alongside the current one). 3) Periodically confirm the style guide itself is still accurate and complete. |
| Tools | Shared cloud storage |
| Deliverable | A current, well-organized, single source of truth for brand assets |
| Owner | Brand designer |
| Dependency | Ongoing |
| Frequency | Quarterly |
| KPI | No ambiguity about which asset version is current, at any point in time |
| Quality check | Spot-checked that the most recent files are clearly marked as current |
| Common mistake | Leaving multiple versions of the logo in the same folder with no clear indication of which is current, leading another team to use an outdated one by mistake |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

## E. Deliverables

**Initial deliverables**
- Brand discovery notes and competitor visual audit
- Visual direction brief
- Logo concept presentation and finalized full logo system (all variants)
- Finalized color palette and typography, with licensing confirmed
- Core brand templates (social, presentation, print basics as relevant)
- Complete final asset package and brand style guide
- Brand asset handoff summary

**Quarterly deliverables**
- Brand-consistency spot-check across live channels
- Asset library maintenance review

**Optional add-ons**
- Full print collateral suite (menus, signage, packaging)
- Rebrand/refresh project for an existing brand
- Sub-brand or multi-location brand variant development

**Monthly deliverables:** none by default — this is a front-loaded identity project maintained quarterly thereafter, extended as needed (Task 10.1).

## G. Tools

| Category | Tools | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Canva (free tier) for lighter template work, Google Fonts, Coolors (palette exploration) | Workable for a very simple identity project |
| Low-cost | Canva Pro, a lower-cost vector design tool | Improves flexibility for template-based ongoing use |
| Professional | Adobe Illustrator/InDesign | The standard for genuine logo development and print-ready file production; recommended as the primary tool for the core identity work even on a budget-conscious project |
| Low-bandwidth / mobile-first consideration | Final assets exported and compressed appropriately for use across mobile-first channels, alongside full-resolution source files for print/professional use | Ensures the identity performs well everywhere it's actually used |

## H. Risks and Common Failure Modes

- **Technical risk:** delivering only flattened/rasterized files instead of editable vector source files, limiting all future use.
- **Platform risk:** not applicable in the traditional sense, though font licensing terms can restrict certain uses (web embedding vs. print) — confirm license scope before use.
- **Client-related risk:** an unlimited, informal revision process without reference to the contracted scope, or a client's personal taste overriding the evidence-based direction from discovery.
- **Data and access risk:** final source files not properly handed over or accessible to the teams that need them.
- **Reputation risk:** an inconsistent, unresolved, or generic identity undermines every other service's professionalism, since the visual identity is what customers see first across every channel.
- **Security risk:** not typically applicable directly to this service.
- **Legal or compliance risk:** font and any referenced imagery licensing must be confirmed for the client's actual intended use (commercial, web, print).
- **Measurement and attribution risk:** the business value of a strong identity is real but genuinely hard to isolate as a precise number — avoid claiming a specific, precisely measured business outcome from branding work alone.

## I. Standard Operating Procedure

- [ ] Brand discovery completed with specific, non-generic answers on story, values, and target customer
- [ ] Competitor visual audit completed
- [ ] Visual direction (personality, color, typography) approved before logo concepting
- [ ] Design project and future asset-library structure organized from the start
- [ ] Genuinely distinct logo concepts presented, not minor variations of one idea
- [ ] Full logo system, finalized color palette, and typography developed with all practical variants
- [ ] Core templates applied and checked for genuine reusability
- [ ] Logo tested for small-size legibility and single-color reproduction; palette tested for accessibility contrast
- [ ] Final identity approved within the agreed revision-round limit
- [ ] Complete asset package delivered, including editable vector source files and licensing information
- [ ] Brand asset handoff summary delivered with confirmed access for every relevant team
- [ ] Live-channel consistency spot-checked quarterly
- [ ] System extended as new needs arise, checked against the style guide each time
- [ ] Asset library kept organized with a single, unambiguous current version of every file
