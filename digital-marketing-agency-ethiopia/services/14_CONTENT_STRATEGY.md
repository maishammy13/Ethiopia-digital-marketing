# Service: Content Strategy

## A. Service Definition

**What it is.** Defines what a business's content should be about, why, in what formats, and on what calendar structure — before any individual post is written, photographed, or filmed. Produces content pillars, content objectives, a format mix per platform, and a repeatable topic bank that [15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md) and [13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md](13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md) build from.

**What business problem it solves.** Accounts that post randomly, with no throughline connecting individual pieces to business goals, don't build a recognizable presence and don't reliably produce a business result. "Post something regularly" is an activity, not a strategy.

**Why a customer would pay for it.** A deliberate content strategy connects every post to a real objective (awareness, trust, leads, retention) and produces a standing topic bank, so production isn't scrambling for ideas each week and every piece of content has a reason to exist.

**Business types that benefit most.** Any business investing in ongoing content production and management, especially those needing to build trust or recognition over many touchpoints — see the "Social Media" and "Visual-content suitability" dimensions across [../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md](../market-mapping/06_SERVICE_TO_SEGMENT_MATCHING.md) and [../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md](../market-mapping/02_CUSTOMER_SEGMENT_PRIORITIZATION.md).

**What this does not include.** Account setup ([12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md](12_SOCIAL_MEDIA_ACCOUNT_SETUP.md)), the actual writing, filming, or design of individual pieces of content ([15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md](15_CONTENT_PRODUCTION.md)), or scheduling, publishing, and community management ([13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md](13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md)).

**How it connects to other services.** Consumes personas from [01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md](01_WEBSITE_STRATEGY.md)/[../market-mapping/04_BUYER_PERSONAS.md](../market-mapping/04_BUYER_PERSONAS.md) and brand guidelines from [25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md](25_BRANDING_AND_GRAPHIC_DESIGN.md). Directly feeds the production queue in 15 and the publishing calendar in 13. Coordinates with [16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md](16_PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_VIDEO.md) for planned shoots needed to fulfill the content pillars.

## B. Desired Business Outcomes

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Activity | Audience/content research, defining pillars/objectives/formats, building a calendar structure and topic bank |
| Output | Content pillar framework, content-objective map, format-mix plan, topic bank, calendar template |
| Outcome | Every piece of content produced has a clear purpose and contributes to a recognizable, consistent presence, rather than being a one-off, disconnected post |
| Financial impact | More efficient production (15 isn't wasting time deciding what to make); content that actually supports business goals instead of just generating activity |

## C. Inputs Required From the Client

- Personas and business goals from 01/market-mapping
- Brand voice and visual guidelines from 25
- Selected platforms from 12
- Any existing content-performance history (from 13, if the account has been active)
- Realistic production constraints: budget, available photography/video capacity, staff time for filming
- Any recurring business events worth planning content around (seasonal offers, launches, festivals relevant to the target customer)

## D. Detailed Task Breakdown

### 1. Preparation

**Task: Confirm business goals and platform scope for the content strategy**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Content strategy must serve specific, agreed goals and the specific platforms actually in scope, not a generic template |
| Inputs | Business goals, platform selection from 12 |
| Procedure | 1) Confirm which business goals content should support (e.g. brand awareness, direct leads, customer retention). 2) Confirm which platforms are in scope and any platform-specific goal differences. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Confirmed goals and platform scope |
| Owner | Content strategist |
| Dependency | 12, Task 8.1 |
| Frequency | One-time per client |
| KPI | Goals and scope confirmed before research begins |
| Quality check | Goals are specific enough to shape actual content decisions, not generic ("build our brand") |
| Common mistake | Proceeding with a one-size-fits-all content plan without confirming what this specific business actually needs content to achieve |
| Estimated complexity | Low |

### 2. Research

|---|---|

| Inputs | Personas from 01/market-mapping |

| Deliverable | Audience content-preference notes |

| Dependency | Task 1.1 |

| Common mistake | Assuming the target audience wants the same content style the business owner personally prefers, rather than researching the actual audience |

### 3. Strategy

**Task: Define content objectives per stage**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Not all content should try to sell directly — a healthy content mix serves awareness, trust, and conversion at different points |
| Inputs | Business goals, audience research |
| Procedure | 1) Define what proportion/type of content builds awareness (reaching new people), what builds trust (showing expertise, authenticity, social proof), and what drives direct action (an offer, a clear call-to-action). 2) Avoid an all-promotional content mix, which tends to underperform and feel repetitive. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Content-objective framework |
| Owner | Content strategist |
| Dependency | Task 1.1 |
| Frequency | One-time, revisited quarterly |
| KPI | Framework includes a defined, non-all-promotional mix |
| Quality check | Reviewed against the audience research for genuine fit, not a generic ratio applied blindly |
| Common mistake | Making every single post a direct sales pitch, which tends to reduce engagement and follower trust over time |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

**Task: Define content pillars**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Pillars are the recurring themes that give the account a recognizable identity and make idea generation repeatable rather than starting from zero each time |
| Inputs | Business offerings, audience research, competitor audit |
| Procedure | 1) Define 3–5 recurring content pillars specific to this business (e.g. for a restaurant: menu highlights, behind-the-scenes, customer moments, local/cultural relevance). 2) Confirm each pillar connects to a real audience interest and a business goal. |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Content pillar framework |
| Owner | Content strategist |
| Dependency | Task 3.1 |
| Frequency | One-time, revisited quarterly or when the business's offerings change |
| KPI | 3–5 pillars defined, each with a stated rationale |
| Quality check | Each pillar reviewed for whether it can realistically sustain ongoing content, not just a one-off idea |
| Common mistake | Defining pillars too broadly ("food," "lifestyle") to actually guide specific content decisions |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

**Task: Define the format mix per platform**
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Different platforms and formats serve different purposes — a strategy should specify what format supports which pillar and objective |
| Inputs | Selected platforms, pillars, objectives |
| Procedure | 1) For each platform, decide the primary format mix (e.g. short-form video, photo carousel, Stories, static graphics). 2) Match formats to pillars and objectives (e.g. behind-the-scenes pillar suits short-form video; menu highlights suit photo). |
| Tools | Shared document |
| Deliverable | Format-mix plan per platform |
| Owner | Content strategist |
| Dependency | Task 3.2 |
| Frequency | One-time, revisited quarterly |
| KPI | Every pillar has at least one assigned format per relevant platform |
| Quality check | Format choices checked against realistic production capacity (Task 1.1 constraints) |
| Common mistake | Planning a format mix (e.g. heavy video) the client's actual production capacity can't sustain |
| Estimated complexity | Medium |

## H. Risks and Common Failure Modes

- **Technical risk:** minimal directly; mainly a planning discipline rather than a technical one.
- **Platform risk:** a format mix planned around a platform feature that changes or is deprecated — revisit per the quarterly best-practice review in [13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md](13_SOCIAL_MEDIA_MANAGEMENT.md) Task 2.1.
- **Client-related risk:** a client repeatedly requesting off-strategy, purely promotional content that undermines the intended pillar/objective balance — explain the trade-off rather than silently complying every time.
- **Data and access risk:** planning a format mix (e.g. heavy video) the client can't realistically supply source material for.
- **Reputation risk:** a content strategy that ignores real audience research in favor of what the business owner personally likes tends to underperform and can feel inauthentic to real customers.
- **Security risk:** not typically applicable directly to this service.
- **Legal or compliance risk:** pillar and topic ideas for ⚠️-flagged sectors must be checked against the same claim restrictions as copywriting before being handed to production.
- **Measurement and attribution risk:** content performance is shared across strategy, production quality, and management execution — avoid attributing a result entirely to the strategy layer alone.

## I. Standard Operating Procedure

- [ ] Business goals and platform scope confirmed before research begins
- [ ] Audience content-preference research and competitor content audit completed
- [ ] Content-objective framework defined with a non-all-promotional mix
- [ ] 3–5 content pillars defined, each with a stated rationale
- [ ] Format mix per platform defined and checked against realistic production capacity
- [ ] Calendar template and topic-bank system built and confirmed usable by production/management teams
- [ ] Initial topic/idea bank generated, covering at least one full planning cycle
- [ ] Content calendar built for the period, balanced across pillars/objectives
- [ ] Calendar peer-reviewed for balance and production feasibility
- [ ] Client approval obtained before production begins
- [ ] Pillar/format performance monitored monthly using 13's data
- [ ] Pillar/format mix refreshed quarterly based on evidence
- [ ] Quarterly content strategy summary delivered
- [ ] Topic bank replenished ahead of depletion; pillars kept current as the business evolves
